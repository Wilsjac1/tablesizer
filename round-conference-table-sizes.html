Round conference table sizes

A round table seats its circumference divided by the space per person. There is no head of the table, which suits smaller, equal-sided conversations — and caps how many people fit before the diameter outgrows the room.

The round rule

Seats = circumference ÷ space per person, rounded down (Paul Downs seating guide). That reproduces the published checks used across this site: at 36 inches a 48-inch round seats four and a 96-inch round seats eight. Because the diameter grows with every added seat, rounds are practical roughly from four to twelve people; beyond that, a rectangle or boat shape seats more people in less room.

Choosing a diameter

  • 4–6 people: a 48–72 inch round keeps everyone within easy conversation distance.
  • 8–10 people: allow 96–120 inches at comfortable spacing, and check the square room it needs.
  • 12 people: the top of the practical range — compare a rectangle in the planner before committing.

Questions

Conference table sizing, answered

How many people fit at a 72-inch round table?

At 36 inches per person, a 72-inch round seats 6: its circumference divided by the space per person (Paul Downs).

What diameter round table seats 10 people?

At 36 inches per person, allow a 120-inch (10 ft) diameter; at 30 inches per person, 96 inches.

Round or rectangle for a small meeting room?

Rectangles seat the most people per foot. Round tables seat their circumference divided by the space per person, and suit smaller, equal-sided conversations where nobody sits at a head.

How much room does a round table need?

Add 42–48 inches of clearance on every side of the diameter; the chart shows the minimum square room at 36 inches per person.
