Reception desk dimensions

A reception desk has two working heights: a higher counter for standing visitors and a lower surface where staff sit and work. Size the width for the number of staff, then leave clear space in front and behind.

Reception desk height

The transaction counter, the top that visitors lean on and hand things across, is usually 40 to 42 inches high. It also screens the receptionist's screen and paperwork. The work surface where staff sit is 28 to 34 inches, the same range the ADA uses for work surfaces, and most are built at 29 to 30 inches to match a standard office chair (ArcGrove; 2010 ADA Standards §902.3). That is the same seated height as a conference table; see conference table height and clearance.

Reception desk width: one or two people

One receptionist needs 60 to 96 inches of desk; most single stations run 84 to 96 inches so a monitor, phone and paperwork fit side by side. Two people, or one person with a lot of equipment, want about 120 inches on a straight desk (ArcGrove). If you need two stations but lack the width, an L adds a 40 to 54 inch return, and a U wraps the work surface around three sides.

Keep the approach clear so visitors reach the counter without crossing staff circulation. The 36 and 48 inch figures behind the desk are the same circulation rule we use behind conference chairs in the conference room layout guide.

Accessibility is part of the dimension

Where the public is served, at least one section of counter must be no higher than 36 inches: at least 36 inches long for a parallel approach, or 30 inches long with knee and toe space for a forward approach (2010 ADA Standards §904.4). California applies a 34-inch maximum and asks for 36 inches of length for both approaches, according to OfficeSimpli. In practice this means a two-level desk: the main counter at 40 to 42 inches plus a lowered section at desk height at one end, which adds its length to the footprint.

Questions

Reception desk sizing, answered

What are standard reception desk dimensions?

A one-person straight reception desk is typically 60 to 96 inches wide and 30 to 36 inches deep, with a 40 to 42 inch transaction counter and a 28 to 34 inch seated work surface (ArcGrove; OfficeSimpli).

How tall is a front desk?

The visitor-facing counter of a front or reception desk is usually 40 to 42 inches high. The seated work surface behind it is usually 28 to 34 inches; 29 to 30 inches is common.

What are 2 person reception desk dimensions?

A straight desk for two people is about 120 inches wide and 30 to 36 inches deep. A U-shaped two-station desk runs about 72 to 120 inches wide and 98 to 110 inches deep (ArcGrove).

How wide is an L-shaped reception desk?

L-shaped desks commonly measure about 90 to 110 inches front to back, with a return of 40 to 54 inches that creates a second work zone (ArcGrove).

What does the ADA require at a reception desk?

Where the public is served, one counter section must be no higher than 36 inches and at least 36 inches long, or 30 inches long with knee and toe space for a forward approach, plus a 30 by 48 inch clear floor space (2010 ADA Standards 904.4 and 305).

How much clearance is needed in front of and behind a reception desk?

In front, keep at least a 30 by 48 inch clear floor space at the accessible section and a clear route of at least 36 inches (ADA 305 and 403.5.1). Behind, allow 36 inches for one person to pass a seated receptionist and 48 inches for two people to pass (CubicleByDesign).
